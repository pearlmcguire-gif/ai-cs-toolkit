# Save Play: At-Risk Account Skill

## Purpose

Give CSMs a structured, diagnostic response to an at-risk account instead of an instinctive discount offer. Based on general industry-standard retention practice, intended to be adapted to a real client's approval limits and offer types.

## Step 1: Diagnose Before Offering Anything

Before proposing any retention offer, identify the actual root cause using the account's own signals:

- **Adoption failure**: usage declining, low feature breadth, no expansion of use case since onboarding.
- **Relationship failure**: No executive engagement, champion has left, no recent EBR.
- **Value failure**: customer can't articulate ROI, or the original success criteria were never actually met.
- **Price failure**: genuinely a budget/procurement issue unrelated to product experience.

Only price failure should lead with a discount conversation. The other three need a plan that fixes the underlying issue, a discount on top of an adoption or trust problem usually just delays the same churn conversation to the next renewal.

## Step 2: Rebuild the Stakeholder Map

If the champion has gone quiet or left, identify who else at the account has a stake in the outcome (their manager, an end-user power user, finance if there's a budget angle) and re-enter through them rather than continuing to wait on an unresponsive contact.

## Step 3: Re-Articulate Value in the Customer's Terms

Pull the account's own usage/outcome data and connect it back to whatever business goal they originally stated at onboarding or the last QBR. Generic "here's what our product does" messaging doesn't move an account that's already decided the value isn't there.

## Step 4: Executive Escalation

Any account above a defined ARR threshold, or any account that has explicitly stated intent to cancel, should get an exec-to-exec touchpoint, CS leadership or the exec sponsor, not just the CSM, in addition to the working-level save plan.

## Step 5: Offer Parameters (if warranted)

If a retention offer is warranted, define it explicitly: what's being offered (discount, extended term, added capability), the approval authority required, and the expiration date on the offer. Undefined or open-ended offers tend to get renegotiated repeatedly.

## Output

A one-page save plan: diagnosed root cause, stakeholder plan, value narrative, escalation status, and (if applicable) the specific offer and its approval status.
